Brilliant review (2026): 2 free lessons a day, then Premium

By the CourseGauge editorial team

Last updated November 20, 2026

Short answer: Brilliant Free gives you 2 keys a day. Each key opens one concept or one practice set, then you wait until midnight. Premium removes that cap. On 20 Nov 2026 the subscribe page showed annual list prices of $20 a month for one person and $40 a month for a family of 6, with a Black Friday price of $14 and $28.

Key facts

Pricing modelPremium is sold monthly, yearly and as a family plan for up to 6 people. A free account remains.
PriceAnnual list price on the subscribe page, 20 Nov 2026: individual $20 a month and family $40 a month for 6 seats, each billed as one yearly payment. The same page showed a Black Friday 30% discount at $14 and $28 a month. The page data also included a $30 monthly individual amount with that seasonal discount attached. A monthly family dollar amount was not printed in the view we checked, so we are not repeating the $60 figure from 15 Oct as current.
IncludedPremium: unlimited lessons and practice, full access to the Koji tutor, no ads, the ability to jump to any lesson, on web, iOS and Android.
Free option2 keys a day. Each key unlocks one concept or one practice set and the keys reset at midnight in your time zone. You have to take topics in order, ads can appear between concepts, and Koji is only a limited preview.
Free trialNo trial length was shown on the 20 Nov subscribe page. Brilliant's help index says trials are sometimes offered.
CertificatesNot advertised on the subscribe page or the pricing help articles we checked.

Who it suits

People who like short problem-solving sessions in math, science, computer science or data, and who will bump into the 2-key wall. Two concepts a day is a real habit. It is a slow way to finish a course. The family plan at the $40 a month annual list price is $480 a year for 6 seats, or $80 a seat, which is the number to compare with buying separate individual plans.

Who should look elsewhere

Anyone who needs a certificate, a coding career path, or several hours of new material in one evening. Free Brilliant will stop you after two concepts. If you want to write code in a browser, Codecademy is the closer match. See Brilliant vs Codecademy.

What two keys a day allows

Brilliant's free-versus-Premium article, last updated 30 Oct 2026 and re-read on 20 Nov, is specific. A free account gets 2 keys each day. One key opens one concept or one practice set. When both keys are used, you stop until midnight in your time zone. You also cannot skip ahead, and ads can sit between concepts. Koji, the tutor inside the lessons, is a preview that runs out.

Premium, on the same article and on the pricing article (also updated 30 Oct 2026), drops the keys, drops the ads, lets you open any lesson, and includes Koji for the session. Both free and Premium can use the lessons on the web, iOS and Android. The pricing article no longer prints dollar amounts. It tells you to open the subscribe page, which is where the figures below come from.

A seat on the family annual list is $480 divided by 6, or $80 a year. That is a third of the individual annual list ($240). It only helps if several people in the house will use it. One person should ignore the family row.

For a credential or a programming path, this is the wrong tool. Khan Academy is free for school math. Brilliant vs Codecademy is the comparison if the goal is writing code. Prices for the wider set sit in the price tracker.

Our take

Stay on the free plan until the 2-key limit annoys you. If you then want a year, the list price we could read was $240 ($20 times 12). During the Black Friday display on 20 Nov that annual charge was shown as $14 a month, which is $168 for the year. Treat $168 as a sale price, not as the renewal price. Brilliant's help centre says it rarely discounts Premium, and names the annual Black Friday and Cyber Monday sale as the main exception.
