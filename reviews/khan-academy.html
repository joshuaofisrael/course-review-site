Khan Academy review (2026): free library, no ads

By the CourseGauge editorial team

Last updated November 21, 2026

Short answer: Khan Academy's help article, updated 20 Sep 2021 and re-read on 21 Nov 2026, still says the library is free: videos, articles, practice questions and lessons, with no ads and no contract. The Khanmigo homepage the same day offered teachers a free sign-up and did not print a price for learners or parents.

Key facts

Pricing modelThe core library is free. The help article says Khan Academy is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, supported by donations and grants.
Free optionVideos, articles, practice questions and lessons are free. The article says there are no ads, no spam and no contracts. It was last updated 20 Sep 2021 and the wording was unchanged when we opened it on 21 Nov 2026.
KhanmigoA separate AI tutor. On khanmigo.ai on 21 Nov 2026, the teacher section said Sign up for free. The learner and parent sections said Get Khanmigo and did not show a dollar amount. We are not quoting a learner price.
CertificatesThe free-library article does not describe a certificate. This is not a credential product.

Who it suits

A student, a parent, or an adult rebuilding school maths or science who wants the lessons without a checkout. The article's claim is broad: the library is free for anyone. That is the fact to use when a paid platform's free tier is only a first chapter. The comparison is Khan Academy vs Coursera.

Who should look elsewhere

Someone who needs a certificate an employer will recognise. The help article we read does not offer one. For that, look at Coursera or the free-audit-then-pay model on edX, both in best free online course platforms. Khanmigo may be the right extra for a household, but only after the live page shows the price. It did not show one to us.

The free-library article is short, and it is specific

Khan Academy's "Does it cost money?" article is four sentences and a date. Updated 20 Sep 2021. Re-read 21 Nov 2026. Learning is "always free." The library of videos, articles, practice questions and lessons is "completely free for anyone." No contracts, no spam, no ads. The organisation is a 501(c)(3), funded by individuals and grants. There is no price table because the article's answer is that there isn't a price.

That is a different free from Alison, where the course is free and the certificate is a shop item, and from edX, where audit access is temporary and the certificate is the paid track. Khan Academy's article does not describe a paid upgrade of the same lessons. If a certificate is the point of the search, this is the wrong free.

Khanmigo is a second product, and the homepage had no price

On 21 Nov we opened khanmigo.ai. Teachers were told to sign up for free. Learners were told Khanmigo will not hand over the answer, and were sent to Get Khanmigo. Parents got the same button. Nowhere on that page was a dollar sign. We did not create an account, and we are not filling the blank with a number from somewhere else.

The practical split: use the free library for the subject itself. Treat Khanmigo as optional, and only after its own screen shows what it costs for the account you have. A teacher account and a parent account are not the same button. For a paid daily-practice alternative with a published cap, see Brilliant and the price tracker.

Compare current prices for all platforms in our price and free-trial tracker, check whether an annual plan pays off with the monthly vs annual calculator, or take the platform quiz.

Our take

Start on the free library. Do not pay another site for school maths until you have seen whether Khan Academy already teaches it. If a page is selling Khanmigo, read the number on that page. The homepage we opened did not provide one, and a review should not invent it. Paid problem-solving of a different kind is Brilliant, which meters a free account at two lessons a day.
