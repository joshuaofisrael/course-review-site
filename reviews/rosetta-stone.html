Rosetta Stone review (2026): 25 languages, $159 a year

By the CourseGauge editorial team

Last updated November 20, 2026

Short answer: Rosetta Stone's plans page, read on 20 Nov 2026, charges $19.99 a month or $159 for the year, and that subscription includes all 25 languages. The yearly plan costs less than eight months of the monthly plan. A lifetime subscription is still described in a support article, but neither that article nor the plans page printed a lifetime price.

Key facts

Pricing modelMonthly or yearly subscription for one person. A lifetime subscription is described in Rosetta Stone's support centre, without a price on the pages we checked.
Price$19.99 a month billed monthly, or $13.25 a month billed yearly as $159. The plans page labels the yearly option as save 33%.
Free optionNot listed on the plans page we checked on 20 Nov 2026.
FormatImmersion lessons, the TruAccent speech tool, Chat Missions for conversation practice, 500+ flashcard sets, and Sapphire Studio for making your own materials. The plans page says one subscription covers all 25 languages.
LanguagesThe plans page lists Spanish (Spain), Spanish (Latin America), German, French, Italian, Dutch, Arabic, Korean, Swedish, Japanese, Hebrew, Chinese, Greek, Persian, Turkish, Russian, Vietnamese, Hindi, Portuguese (Brazil), Filipino (Tagalog), Irish, Latin, English (American), English (British) and Polish.
LifetimeThe support article dated 12 Jan 2026 says a lifetime subscription lasts while the products and services you bought remain available and supported, that each license is for one person, and that it cannot be transferred. It does not state a price.
CertificatesNot advertised on the plans page we checked.

Who it suits

A learner who wants several languages on one bill and likes immersion without translation drills. $159 a year for 25 languages is the clean comparison with Babbel, whose review page prices 12 months at $107.40 and then argues with its own help centre about whether that plan is one language or all of them.

Who should look elsewhere

A tight budget, or anyone who wants a certificate. There is no free tier on the plans page we opened. Nineteen dollars and ninety-nine cents a month with no printed refund window is an expensive way to discover you will not keep the habit. Try Duolingo first if you are unsure.

Eight months is the break-even

The plans page on 20 Nov 2026 showed two numbers and a language list, not a maze of promo codes. Monthly is $19.99, charged every month. Yearly is $159, shown as $13.25 a month, with a "save 33%" label. Twelve times $19.99 is $239.88. The difference is $80.88, which is 33.7% off the monthly run-rate. That is the label, rounded.

Divide 159 by 19.99 and you get 7.95. From the eighth month, the yearly plan has cost less than staying on monthly. If you might quit in month three, monthly is the smaller loss, and only if you actually cancel. The plans page we read did not state a refund window.

What the $159 includes

The same page says the subscription is all-access: all 25 languages, not a pick-one model. It names immersive lessons, TruAccent, Chat Missions, more than 500 flashcard sets, and Sapphire Studio. The 25 names on the page are Spanish from Spain, Spanish from Latin America, German, French, Italian, Dutch, Arabic, Korean, Swedish, Japanese, Hebrew, Chinese, Greek, Persian, Turkish, Russian, Vietnamese, Hindi, Portuguese from Brazil, Filipino (Tagalog), Irish, Latin, American English, British English and Polish.

Set that beside Babbel. Babbel's review page, the same day, priced 12 months at $107.40 and lifetime at $299.99, and said each language is a separate subscription unless you buy lifetime. Rosetta Stone's plans page does not make you have that argument: 25 languages are in the subscription description. Babbel is cheaper for a year if one language is all you want. Rosetta Stone is the simpler bill if you want more than one. The three-way version is best language learning apps.

Lifetime, without a price

Rosetta Stone's lifetime article, bylined 12 Jan 2026, is still up. It says lifetime means access for as long as the products and services you purchased remain available and supported, that the license is for one person, and that it cannot be transferred. It tells you to read the terms. It does not say how many dollars lifetime costs today, and the plans page we opened did not show a lifetime box either. If a lifetime offer appears at checkout, the price on that screen is the price. We do not have a second number to quote.

Our take

The yearly plan is the one that matches how the product is priced. $159 divided by $19.99 is 7.95, so the annual bill costs less than paying monthly once you are in month 8. The plans page's "save 33%" line also checks: twelve months at $19.99 is $239.88, and $159 is about 34% less. Do not buy a lifetime plan from a third-party seller on the strength of the support article. That article defines the term and does not list a current price.
