Babbel review (2026): 14 languages, 12-month plan $107.40

By the CourseGauge editorial team

Last updated November 20, 2026

Short answer: Babbel's own review page, read on 20 Nov 2026, still prices a 12-month plan at $107.40 ($8.95 a month), six months at $80.70 and lifetime at $299.99. Full lessons are paid and ad-free. The first lesson of every course is free, and Babbel Live is no longer sold to individual learners.

Key facts

Pricing modelSubscriptions paid in advance for a set period, plus a one-time Lifetime plan. Babbel's pricing help article says the price also depends on your country and on whether you buy from Babbel, a partner site, Google or Apple.
PriceOn Babbel's review page, 20 Nov 2026: 6 months at $13.45 a month ($80.70 billed each 6 months), 12 months at $8.95 a month ($107.40 for the year), Lifetime $299.99 once. The page says 1-month and 3-month plans exist but does not print those totals. The $17.95 and $45.75 figures from our 15 Oct read of the prices app were not re-confirmed, because that page returned no prices without JavaScript.
Free optionRegistration is free, and the first lesson of every course is free to try. Full courses need a subscription. Babbel says the lessons carry no ads.
FormatLessons of about 10 to 15 minutes, built around practical conversation, written for the learner's native language. 14 languages.
CertificatesNo course certificate on the pages we checked.
RenewalRenews for the same period unless you cancel before the renewal date. You keep access until the time you already paid for ends. Deleting the app does not cancel the subscription. The help articles we read do not state a money-back window.

Who it suits

Adults who want short, structured dialogue lessons and will actually open the app most weeks. At the 12-month price on Babbel's review page, a year is $107.40, which is less than Rosetta Stone's $159 yearly plan, with the caveat below about how many languages that Babbel plan includes.

Who should look elsewhere

Anyone who needs a free full course, a certificate, or a live teacher. Babbel's review page says Babbel Live is no longer available for individual consumers. It is also a poor fit if you want dozens of languages in one obvious bundle: see the disagreement between Babbel's own pages, described under the next heading.

Two official pages disagree on languages

This is the part worth slowing down for. Babbel's review page, read on 20 Nov 2026, says each language needs its own subscription unless you buy lifetime access. The subscriptions help article describes an "All languages" subscription for the whole self-study catalog, and lists Lifetime as a separate choice. Both pages are Babbel's. We are not going to pretend they say the same thing. Open the checkout and read whether the plan name is one language or all 14 before you enter a card.

The 14 languages named on the review page are Spanish (Spain and Mexico are separate courses), French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Polish, Turkish, Russian, Indonesian and English. An English speaker learning Spanish does not get the same course as a French speaker learning Spanish: Babbel says each course is written for the learner's native language.

What you can try without paying

The free-lesson article says you can register at no cost and open the first lesson of every course. That is enough to hear whether the dialogue style suits you. It is not a free catalog. The same article says Babbel runs no ads, and it credits a team of over 150 experts for the courses. We are citing that as Babbel's description, not as an independent headcount.

If you wanted a teacher on a video call, stop here. Babbel's review page says Babbel Live is no longer available for individual consumers. For the free-versus-paid choice, read Babbel vs Duolingo, then best language learning apps, which adds Rosetta Stone. The monthly vs annual calculator still shows the 15 Oct one-month price beside the 12-month total, and the note on that row says the one-month figure was not re-read.

Our take

Read the checkout label before you pay for a year. If it is one language, lifetime at $299.99 is the only Babbel product their review page describes as covering every language, and it costs about 2.8 years of the 12-month plan. If the checkout says all languages, the 12-month plan is the simpler buy and lifetime is a bet that you will still be using Babbel in year three. Start with the free first lesson, or with Duolingo if you are not ready to pay.
