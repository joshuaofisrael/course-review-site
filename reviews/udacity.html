Udacity review (2026): $249 a month on the support FAQ

By the CourseGauge editorial team

Last updated November 20, 2026

Short answer: Udacity's support FAQ, re-read on 20 Nov 2026, still says the subscription is $249 a month, or $846 if you pay for four months up front. You cannot buy a single Nanodegree on its own. The public plans page did not print a dollar amount in the page we fetched. It described a monthly plan and a 4-month bundle that converts to month-to-month.

Key facts

Pricing modelOne subscription for the catalog. The support FAQ prices it at $249 a month, or $846 paid up front for four months (it calls that a 15% saving). The plans page, the same day, left the dollar figures as "Loading" and said a discount applies to the first 4 months, after which the plan converts to month-to-month.
Free optionNo free catalog on the FAQ or the plans page. The FAQ says personalised offers and periodic sales exist for some learners.
What you cannot buyThe FAQ says Udacity does not support enrollment in an individual Nanodegree or course. The subscription is the product.
CatalogThe FAQ describes over 80 Nanodegree programs, about 2 to 6 months each depending on weekly hours, and over 300 courses that can often be finished in under a month. The catalog changes: content is added and removed during an active subscription.
CertificatesA Nanodegree certificate comes after you pass the program's projects. The plans page calls the credential a Udacity-verified certification and says it is included on individual, team and enterprise plans.
PauseThe FAQ says you can pause the subscription when you have met a goal, and come back later. It does not describe a refund window.

Who it suits

Someone who can put real weeks into one Nanodegree and wants project reviews, not just videos. At the FAQ's four-month price, $846 is the bill for a program the FAQ itself says often takes two to six months. Four months of $249 would be $996, and $846 is $150 less, which is the 15% the FAQ claims (150 divided by 996).

Who should look elsewhere

Casual learners, and anyone comparing it with a broad catalog subscription. Coursera Plus was $399 for a year at our last full read of that plan. Udacity's four-month FAQ price is already more than twice that, and it does not become a cheap second year: the plans page says the 4-month deal converts to month-to-month. If you only need one Python course, look at Codecademy or a single Udemy course instead.

The support FAQ and the plans page do not match in form

We opened both on 20 Nov 2026. The subscription FAQ is blunt: a monthly subscription is $249, and paying $846 up front for four months saves 15%. The same article later says students subscribe monthly or annually. It does not explain how "annual" relates to the four-month price. We are leaving both wordings here so the conflict is visible.

The plans page described three products in the HTML we could read: a monthly subscription, a 4-month subscription it called the best value, and team plans. The dollar slots said "Loading". Two footnotes were readable. A discount applies to the first 4 months, then the plan converts to month-to-month. The 4-month total is framed around the average time to complete a Nanodegree. Individual plans include the content library, hands-on projects, human project feedback, and a Udacity-verified certification, according to the comparison table on that page.

The arithmetic on Udacity's own four-month claim: 249 times 4 is 996, and 996 minus 846 is 150, which is 15.1% off. That checks out against the "save 15%" line. It is a discount on the first four months, not a permanent rate, if the plans-page footnote is the rule the checkout uses.

The FAQ's catalog description is the other concrete figure. It says there are over 80 Nanodegree programs, usually about two to six months, and over 300 shorter courses. A Nanodegree certificate requires passing the projects. You can be enrolled in more than one thing at a time while the subscription is active. If you finish early, the FAQ says you can pause rather than keep paying. It does not promise a refund of the months you did not use.

Our take

Treat $249 and $846 as the prices Udacity's own support FAQ still publishes, not as a number we saw painted on the plans page on 20 Nov. Open udacity.com/plans and read the checkout, because the FAQ also mentions personalised offers. If the four-month bundle is what you want, finish inside those four months. The footnote on the plans page says the discount applies to the first 4 months and then the membership goes month-to-month.
