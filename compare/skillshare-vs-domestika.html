Skillshare vs Domestika

By the CourseGauge editorial team

Last updated November 21, 2026

Short answer: Skillshare is the cheaper year if you want a pile of short classes and you are happy to stop when you cancel. Domestika Plus costs more, and a course you redeem with a credit is yours after the subscription ends.

MasterClass vs Skillshare

The two bills, read on 21 Nov 2026

Skillshare's pricing page showed one consumer number: $167.88 a year, displayed as $13.99 a month and paid annually. It lists classes, no ads, learning paths, and certificates and badges. It does not say you keep a class after you cancel. Domestika's Plus page showed $33.90 a month, or $20.59 a month billed as $246.75 for the year, with renewal at $329. Twelve times $33.90 is $406.80. The first-year $246.75 is $160.05 less than that, about 39% off the monthly run-rate. The $329 renewal is $77.80 less, about 19% off, which matches the page's own "19% discount" sentence better than the first-year price does.

What you keep

Domestika is explicit. Plus members can watch the catalog while they subscribe. Keeping a course takes a credit: 12 a year on the annual plan, or one a month on the monthly plan. Credits expire a year after you receive them, or when you stop being a Plus member, whichever comes first. Once a course is redeemed, the page says it stays, including the lessons and the private community, even if you cancel. The certificate is for a course you purchased or redeemed, not for pressing play. Skillshare's page describes a membership for watching classes. It does not describe a keep-forever download.

The catalog counts do not agree with each other

Domestika's Plus page, on the same day, said "over +4,000 courses" in the headline and "3,900+ in total" in the feature list. We are not picking one. Skillshare's pricing page said 9,000+ teachers and did not give a class count in the text we read. The products differ in length as well as in price: Skillshare's own pitch is short creative classes, and Domestika's pitch is longer courses from working professionals, many of them produced in Spanish with subtitles. A certificate on either one is the platform's certificate. Neither page calls it a degree.

Frequently asked questions

Is Skillshare or Domestika cheaper for a year?

Skillshare, on the pages we read on 21 Nov 2026. Skillshare was $167.88 for the year. Domestika Plus was $246.75 the first year and $329 after that. The gap in year one is $78.87. In a renewal year it is $161.12, if neither price moves. Skillshare's page showed the annual price only. Domestika also offered $33.90 billed every month.

Do I keep the courses if I cancel?

On Domestika, only the courses you redeemed with a Plus credit. The Plus page says those stay forever, and the ones you only watched do not have to. Skillshare's pricing page does not say a class remains yours after the membership ends.

Does the certificate come with every class I watch?

Not on Domestika. The Plus page says you get the certificate when you complete a course you purchased. Watching it inside the subscription is not the same step. Skillshare's pricing page lists certificates and badges as part of the membership and does not describe them as accredited.

Which one is the MasterClass alternative?

Both sit on our MasterClass alternatives list, for different reasons. Skillshare is the short project class. Domestika is the longer course you can keep if you spend a credit. For lecture courses, the same list includes The Great Courses Plus.
